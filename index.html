How I think

Beliefs formed by being wrong first. Click to expand.

Governance before autonomy

Every agentic system in regulated environments teaches the same lesson: autonomy is earned, not assumed. Pilots with broad tool access revealed silent, hard-to-trace failures catastrophic for audit. The strict phase model felt bureaucratic on paper. In production, it was the only thing that made the system trustworthy enough for enterprise sign-off.

Confidence scoring is the product

Every LLM pipeline has a failure rate. The question is whether the system knows when it's failing and routes accordingly. Processing 50K+ documents monthly: pure OCR failed on unstructured content, pure LLM hallucinated on structured tables. The hybrid pipeline worked only when field-level confidence scoring with exception routing was added.

The best AI product is invisible

Users in high-stakes situations don't care about the model. They care about getting the right answer, fast, and understanding it. The "AI-powered" label is a liability if the experience underneath doesn't work. Design the AI as the engine, keep the surface layer simple.

RAG without citation is confident hallucination

In domains where answers have financial or legal consequences, unexplained confidence is actively dangerous. Users in testing challenged answers they couldn't verify and trusted answers they could trace. The citation wasn't a legal feature. It was the trust feature.

Audit trails are the product

Every agentic system for enterprise needs to answer: "what happened, when, and why?" Teams that treat logging as an afterthought discover they can't answer it at the worst moment. The lead enrichment workflow wrote to Gmail drafts, not sends. That one decision unlocked full stakeholder buy-in for the entire automation.

Ship the 80% that runs

The best technical decision and the best organizational decision are rarely the same. The cleanest architecture was rejected not because it was wrong, but because it required too much change too fast. The version that shipped was 80% as elegant. It ran in production. The clean version never did.
